The one combination to avoid

High temperature with strict document mode. You have told the system to stay strictly within the sources while simultaneously encouraging it to make less predictable word choices. The usual result is answers that drift from the source wording and cite less reliably — precisely the behaviour strict mode exists to prevent.

Temperature, in more detail

Of the sampling controls, this is the one actually worth adjusting. At every step the model holds a ranked list of possible next words with probabilities attached. Temperature reshapes that list before one is picked.

ValueBehaviourSuits
0.0 Always the highest-probability word. Fully repeatable — the same question gives the same answer. Extracting exact values; demonstrating reproducibility
0.1 – 0.3 Nearly deterministic, with enough variation to read naturally. Document questions — the recommended range
0.4 – 0.6 Noticeably more varied phrasing; still generally faithful to sources. Explanation and teaching
0.7 – 1.0 Creative. Analogies and examples appear unprompted, some of them invented. Brainstorming, never factual lookup
1.0+ Increasingly incoherent on most local models. Demonstrating what temperature does
A demonstration worth doing

Set temperature to 0.0 and ask the same question three times — identical answers. Set it to 1.0 and repeat — three different answers. Students routinely assume AI output is either deterministic or arbitrary; seeing that it is a dial someone chose is more informative than any explanation.

Context Length, and the mistake to avoid

Context length is the total working space, measured in tokens — roughly ¾ of a word each. Everything shares it: system instructions, conversation history, retrieved passages, your question, and the answer being generated.

SettingEvidence budgetSuits
2,048~4,900 charactersShort documents only; too tight for real work
8,192 default~19,600 charactersComfortable for most document questions
16,384~39,300 charactersLong synthesis questions across several documents
32,768~78,600 charactersOnly if your model supports it; uses substantial memory

About 60% of this is reserved as the budget for retrieved passages; the remainder holds everything else. That is why lowering it has a direct, unwanted effect on answer quality when documents are loaded.

Do not lower Context Length to gain speed

It is a natural instinct and it makes document answers worse — less of your material reaches the model. To speed things up, turn off optional techniques under Advanced Techniques, switch off follow-up question generation, or use a smaller chat model.

Setting it higher than your model supports is harmless but pointless: the excess is ignored. Check your model's context window before raising it far.

Why two models are needed

Document search requires an embedding model in addition to the chat model. They do different jobs and are not interchangeable.

Chat model

Writes the answer

Reads the retrieved passages and composes a response. Larger models follow the citation instructions more reliably.

ollama pull llama3.1

Embedding model

Makes text searchable

Converts passages and questions into numbers so they can be compared by meaning. Cannot generate text.

ollama pull nomic-embed-text

Without an embedding model the application still runs, documents can still be uploaded and read — but they cannot be searched, and the interface says so rather than answering from general knowledge while appearing to consult your files.

Matching settings to your hardware

8 GB RAM, no GPU 7B or smaller 4,096 All off; follow-ups off
16 GB RAM 7B – 13B 8,192 Re-ranking when needed
32 GB+ or Apple Silicon 13B – 30B 8,192 – 16,384 Any, including contextual headers

For scale: on a mid-range machine a plain document question took around 12 seconds, while the same question with every optional technique enabled took about 40. That difference is the reason the defaults are conservative.

Setup checklist

  1. Install Ollama, then ollama pull nomic-embed-text and one chat model.
  2. Start Ollama, then launch the application.
  3. Confirm the sidebar lists both a chat model and an embedding model.
  4. Set Temperature to 0.2 and leave Top-K and Top-P alone.
  5. Leave Context Length at 8,192.
  6. Upload a document, process it, and confirm the sidebar shows a chunk count.
  7. Ask a question you know the answer to, and open the Sources panel to check the citation points where it should.
Verify before you trust

Step 7 is the one people skip. Asking a question whose answer you already know, then confirming the citation points at the right page, is the fastest way to establish whether the system is working — and the habit worth teaching to anyone who will use it.
